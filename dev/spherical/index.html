Spherical numerics

Latitude quadratures and the Wigner small-d functions, for the packages that sample and transform fields on the sphere.

Latitude quadratures

gauss_legendre! gives the n-point Gauss–Legendre nodes μ = cos θ, their weights and latitudes, exact for polynomials in μ to degree 2n − 1. equiangular_weights! gives the weights of the equiangular colatitudes: Fejér's first rule on OpenNodes, Driscoll & Healy on ClosedNodes. Every set of weights sums to ∫₀^π sin θ dθ = 2.

using FlowTransformBindings: FlowTransformBindings as FTB

gl = FTB.gauss_legendre(6)
(μ = [-0.932469514203152, -0.6612093864662645, -0.2386191860831969, 0.2386191860831969, 0.6612093864662645, 0.932469514203152], w = [0.17132449237917036, 0.3607615730481388, 0.4679139345726909, 0.4679139345726909, 0.3607615730481388, 0.17132449237917036])

Wigner small-d functions

wigner_d! fills d^ℓ_{mn}(θ) for ℓ = 0 … L by the three-term recurrence in ℓ, from the closed form at ℓ = max(|m|, |n|), with values far below their turning point held to a scaled exponent. The primitives it is built from, wigner_d_start, wigner_d_coeffs, the steps and wigner_d_rescale, serve a transform that runs the recurrence over many angles at once.

FlowTransformBindings.gauss_legendre! — Function
gauss_legendre!(T, μ, w, lat, n) -> nothing

The n-point Gauss–Legendre rule on μ ∈ (-1, 1), ascending in μ: the nodes into μ, the weights (summing to 2) into w and the latitudes asin(μ) into lat, each of length n, or nothing where not wanted. At Float64 above n = 60 they come from asymptotic expansions, O(1) per node; otherwise from Newton on Pₙ, O(n) per root.

lat is formed as π/2 − θ from the colatitude θ the solve works in. Near a pole μ rounds next to ±1, where asin amplifies that rounding by 1/sin θ; the colatitude carries no such loss.

FlowTransformBindings.equiangular_weights! — Function
equiangular_weights!(w, nodes, algorithm = Recurrence()) -> w

The latitude weights of the N = length(w) equiangular nodes, from the sine series S_m(θ) = Σ_{k=0}^{m} sin((2k+1)θ)/(2k+1), which satisfies

sinθ·S_m(θ) = ½ − Σ_{j=1}^{m} cos(2jθ)/(4j²−1) − cos((2m+2)θ)/(2(2m+1)):
  • ClosedNodes, Driscoll & Healy (1994): wᵢ = (4/N)·sinθᵢ·S_m(θᵢ), m = N/2 − 1.
  • OpenNodes, Fejér's first rule wᵢ = (2/N)[1 − 2Σ_{j=1}^{⌊N/2⌋} cos(2jθᵢ)/(4j²−1)]: wᵢ = (4/N)[sinθᵢ·S_m(θᵢ) + cos((2m+2)θᵢ)/(2(2m+1))], m = ⌊N/2⌋.

FlowTransformBindings.wigner_d_coeffs — Function
wigner_d_coeffs(F, ℓ, m, n) -> (α, β, γ, c₊, c₋)

The step d^{ℓ+1} = (αx + β) d^ℓ − γ d^{ℓ−1} from degree ℓ in F, with c₊ = α + β − 1 − γ and c₋ = β − α + 1 + γ. With D = ℓ b, a = √((ℓ²−m²)(ℓ²−n²)), b = √(((ℓ+1)²−m²)((ℓ+1)²−n²)) and (ℓ² ∓ mn)² − a² = ℓ²(m ∓ n)²,

c₊ D = (ℓ+1) ℓ²(m−n)² / (ℓ² − mn + a) + ℓ (ℓ+1)²(m−n)² / ((ℓ+1)² − mn + b),
c₋ D = −(ℓ+1) ℓ²(m+n)² / (ℓ² + mn + a) − ℓ (ℓ+1)²(m+n)² / ((ℓ+1)² + mn + b),

sums of terms of one sign, so each is accurate to eps(F) relative to itself.

FlowTransformBindings.wigner_d_step — Function
wigner_d_step(σα, c, σγ, σ, u, y, e) -> (y′, e′)

One degree up in Reinsch's form: e′ = (c − σα u) y + σγ e, y′ = σ y + e′. At ℓ0, e = y.
